
## What IFRS 1 is for?

IFRS 1 aims to ensure that an entity's first IFRS financial statements:

- Are transparent and comparable across periods.
- Provide a suitable starting point for future IFRS reporting.
- Deliver benefits that outweigh the cost of implementation.

It sets out how an entity moves from its previous accounting framework to IFRS Accounting Standards for its first IFRS financial statements. Its aim is to make the first IFRS information transparent and comparable across the periods presented.


## Step by Step application

| Step | Description | 
| --- | --- |
| **1. Identify the date of transition** | Determine the beginning of the earliest comparative period presented under IFRS in the first IFRS financial statements. | 
| **2. Prepare the opening IFRS statement of financial position** | Recognise, derecognise, reclassify, and measure items as required by IFRS at the transition date. |
| **3. Apply consistent IFRS accounting policies** | Use the policies that comply with IFRS and are effective at the end of the first IFRS reporting period across the periods presented, subject to IFRS 1's specific reliefs and requirements. | 
| **4. Explain the transition** | Provide the reconciliations and other disclosures required by IFRS 1. So users can understand how moving from previous GAAP affected the reported figures. |

## Interlinkages with Other IFRS Standards

IFRS 1 does not operate in isolation, it interacts extensively with numerous other standards:

### 1. IFRS 1 ↔ IFRS 3 (Business Combinations)

- **Exemption**: First-time adopters may elect **not** to restate business combinations that occurred before the date of transition (Appendix C of IFRS 1).
- If the exemption is used, the carrying amounts of assets/liabilities acquired are based on previous GAAP immediately after the combination as **deemed cost**.
- Goodwill is not adjusted for accumulated amortisation but is tested for impairment under **IAS 36**.
- Intangible assets not recognised under previous GAAP may need to be recognised or transferred to goodwill under **IAS 38**.

### 2. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 16 (Property, Plant & Equipment)

- Entities may elect to use **fair value at the date of transition** as deemed cost (paragraph D5).
- Depreciation methods consistent with previous GAAP may be retained unless they materially differ from IFRS requirements (paragraph D8).
- Changes in estimated useful life or depreciation pattern are applied **prospectively**.

### 3. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 36 (Impairment of Assets)

- An entity must test for impairment at the date of transition, based on conditions existing at that date.
- Estimates used are consistent with previous GAAP estimates (adjusted for policy differences), unless there is objective evidence of error.
- Subsequent reversals of impairment losses are recognised in profit or loss.

### 4. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 37 (Provisions, Contingent Liabilities & Contingent Assets)

- IFRS 1 Appendix D, Paragraph D21 provides a first-time adoption exemption related to decommissioning, restoration, and similar liabilities (for example, obligations to dismantle a plant, restore a site, or remove equipment at the end of its useful life) (paragraph D21).
- Under IFRIC 1, when a decommissioning liability changes, the change is normally added to or deducted from the carrying amount of the related asset, and the revised depreciable amount is depreciated **prospectively**.
- For a company adopting IFRS for the first time, reconstructing every historical change in those liabilities can be very difficult. A first-time adopter does not need to retrospectively apply IFRIC 1 to changes in these liabilities that occurred before the transition date, Instead, at the transition date the entity:

    - Measures the liability under IAS 37.
    - Estimates the amount that would originally have been included in the asset's cost when the obligation first arose by discounting the liability using an estimate of the historical risk-adjusted discount rate(s).
    - Calculates accumulated depreciation on that estimated amount up to the transition date using:
        
        - the current estimate of the asset's useful life, and
        - the depreciation policy adopted under IFRS.

### 5. IFRS 1 ↔ IFRS 9 / IAS 32 (Financial Instruments)

- All financial assets and liabilities are recognised and measured in accordance with **IFRS 9** at the date of transition.
- Classification is based on facts and circumstances existing at the transition date.
- **Hedge accounting** can only be applied prospectively from the date the hedge relationship is fully designated and documented hedge accounting (paragraphs B4–B6).
- At the date of transition to IFRS, hedge accounting can only be recognized if the hedging relationship satisfies the hedge accounting requirements of IFRS 9. An entity **cannot retrospectively** create hedge relationships that did not exist or were not properly designated and documented before transition.
- A first-time adopter must not include in its opening IFRS statement of financial position a hedging relationship that does not qualify under IFRS 9. Examples include:
    
    - Certain hedges using a stand-alone written option as the hedging instrument.
    - Some net-position hedges that fail IFRS 9 requirements.

- However, if a net position was designated as a hedged item under previous GAAP, the entity may redesignate an eligible individual item or qualifying net position under IFRS 9 by the transition date.
- If, before transition, an entity had designated a transaction as a hedge and that hedge meets IFRS 9 requirements, the entity generally:

    - Continues hedge accounting from the transition date.
    - Uses the amounts recognized under previous GAAP as the starting point, subject to IFRS requirements.
    - Does not retrospectively designate new hedge relationships or reconstruct documentation.

### 6. IFRS 1 ↔ IFRS 16 (Leases)

- Lessors classify leases based on circumstances at **inception** of the lease.
- First-time adopters have specific transition reliefs (paragraphs D9) for leases.
- a first-time adopter may assess whether a contract contains a lease at the date of transition to IFRSs, rather than reassessing all contracts retrospectively from the contract inception date.
- Instead of reviewing every historical contract under IFRS 16 from the date each contract was entered into, an entity may apply the requirements of IFRS 16 to determine whether an arrangement contains a lease based on the facts and circumstances that exist at the date of transition to IFRSs.
- If a subsidiary was a lessee under operating leases under previous GAAP, IFRS 1 provides a specific relief when the subsidiary adopts IFRS later than its parent. Under IFRS 1 D16(a), a subsidiary may measure its lease liability and right-of-use (ROU) asset ***using amounts that would be included in the parent's consolidated financial statements***, based on the parent's date of transition to IFRS. This exemption was introduced to simplify adoption for subsidiaries joining an IFRS-reporting group later.

### 7. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 12 (Income Taxes)

- Deferred tax is recognised on temporary differences between carrying amounts in the opening IFRS statement of financial position and their tax bases.
- Tax rates reflect laws enacted or substantively enacted by the end of the reporting period.

### 8. IFRS 1 ↔ IFRS 10 (Consolidated Financial Statements)

- All subsidiaries as defined in IFRS 10 are consolidated.
- If a subsidiary was not consolidated under previous GAAP, its assets and liabilities are measured at the same carrying amounts as in its IFRS financial statements (paragraph D17).
- Parent and subsidiary may adopt IFRSs at different dates (paragraphs D16–D17).

### 9. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 21 (Foreign Exchange)

- Entities apply IAS 21 to determine functional and presentation currency.
- Goodwill and fair value adjustments on acquisition of foreign operations may be treated as assets/liabilities of the entity or the foreign operation, with prospective application permitted.

### 10. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 29 (Hyperinflationary Economies)

- If an entity has a functional currency that was, or is, the currency of a hyperinflationary economy, it shall determine whether it was subject to severe hyperinflation before the date of transition to IFRSs. This applies to entities that are adopting IFRSs for the first time, as well as entities that have previously applied IFRSs (paragraphs D26).
- The currency of a hyperinflationary economy is subject to severe hyperinflation if it has both of the following characteristics:

(a) a reliable general price index is not available to all entities with transactions and balances in the currency.
(b) the currency is not exchangeable into a relatively stable foreign currency. Exchangeability is assessed in accordance with IAS 21       
        
### 11. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 2 (Inventories)

- Inventories are measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value.
- Cost includes all costs of purchase, conversion, and other costs incurred in bringing inventories to their present location and condition.

### 12. IFRS 1 ↔ IFRS 15 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers)

- Amounts received that do not yet qualify as revenue are recognised as liabilities, adjusted for significant financing components.

### 13. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 20 (Government Grants)

- Government loans existing at the date of transition use their previous GAAP carrying amount as the IFRS carrying amount.
- IAS 32 is applied to classify the loan, and IFRS 9 is applied subsequently.

### 14. IFRS 1 ↔ IFRS 2 (Share-based Payment)

- First-time adopters are encouraged but not required to apply IFRS 2 to equity instruments granted after 7 November 2002 that vested before the later of the transition date and 1 January 2005.

### 15. IFRS 1 ↔ IAS 34 (Interim Financial Reporting)

- If interim reports are presented for part of the first IFRS period, comparative information must be restated to comply with IFRSs.
- Reconciliations of equity and total comprehensive income are required.

### Case Study

---
Suppose an entity presents its first IFRS financial statements for the year ending **31 December 20X5**, with one year of comparative information for **20X4**. The earliest comparative period begins on **1 January 20X4**, so that is generally the date of transition and the date of the opening IFRS statement of financial position. Always confirm the periods actually presented and the applicable IFRS 1 requirements.

---

A company adopting IFRS for the first time on 1 January 20X5 has hundreds of service and rental contracts entered into over many years.

Without the exemption:

The company would need to assess each contract at inception to determine whether it contains a lease.

Using D9:

The company can assess the contracts based on conditions existing at 1 January 20X5 (the transition date) and determine which contracts contain leases under IFRS 16.

Audit focus

When a client applies the D9 exemption, verify:

The election is applied consistently.
The lease assessment is based on facts and circumstances at the transition date.
Appropriate disclosure of significant IFRS 1 exemptions used in the transition process.
---
Practical effect

Assume:

Parent adopted IFRS on 1 January 2023.
Subsidiary adopts IFRS for its stand-alone financial statements on 1 January 2026.
Under previous GAAP, the lease was treated as an operating lease and therefore was off-balance-sheet.

Without the exemption, the subsidiary would need to apply IFRS 16 and reconstruct lease accounting from commencement or transition requirements.

Using IFRS 1 D16(a), the subsidiary can:

Measure the lease liability at the carrying amount that would have been recognized under IFRS 16 in the parent's consolidated accounts.
Measure the ROU asset on the same basis after adjusting for consolidation effects and acquisition accounting, if any.
If the subsidiary does not use D16(a)

The subsidiary applies the normal first-time adoption requirements of IFRS 1 and IFRS 16. For leases previously classified as operating leases, it generally recognizes:

a lease liability for future lease payments, and
a corresponding right-of-use asset

at the date of transition. IFRS 16 requires lessees to recognize lease assets and liabilities for nearly all leases, including those previously accounted for as operating leases.

Audit point

When reviewing a first-time adopter subsidiary:

Determine whether the parent has already adopted IFRS.
Assess whether the subsidiary elected the D16(a) exemption.
If elected, verify that lease balances are consistent with the amounts that would have been recognized in the parent's IFRS reporting.
If not elected, test the lease transition calculations under IFRS 16.

## Retrospective application: the general principle

As a general starting point, an entity applies IFRS Accounting Standards retrospectively in its opening IFRS statement of financial position and throughout the periods presented, as if it had always applied IFRS. IFRS 1 modifies that principle through specific **optional exemptions** and **mandatory exceptions**. These terms are not interchangeable:

- **Optional exemptions** provide specified transition relief that a first-time adopter may elect to use. They are limited to the circumstances described in IFRS 1 and should not be extended by analogy.
- **Mandatory exceptions** prohibit retrospective application in specified areas, often to avoid hindsight or because applying a requirement retrospectively would be impracticable or inconsistent with the relevant standard's transition rules.

The lists below are illustrative rather than exhaustive; refer to the current standard for the full requirements and conditions.

## Optional exemptions

Examples of areas for which IFRS 1 provides specific optional relief include:

- **Business combinations:** specified relief from retrospective application of IFRS requirements for past business combinations.
- **Deemed cost:** in defined circumstances, an entity may elect a fair value or previous-GAAP revaluation amount as deemed cost for an eligible item at the transition date.
- **Leases:** specified transition relief for lease accounting.
- **Cumulative translation differences:** specified relief for resetting or applying accumulated translation differences.
- **Share-based payment transactions:** specified relief for certain transactions within the scope of IFRS 2.

An election is subject to detailed criteria. An exemption available for one topic does not automatically apply to other transactions or balances.

## Mandatory exceptions

IFRS 1 also requires prospective treatment in certain areas. Examples include:

- **Estimates:** estimates made under IFRS at the transition date must be consistent with estimates made for the same date under previous GAAP, after adjustments for differences in accounting policies, unless there is objective evidence of error.
- **Derecognition of financial assets and liabilities:** the transition provisions restrict retrospective application in specified circumstances.
- **Hedge accounting:** hedge accounting is subject to specific transition restrictions and conditions.
- **Non-controlling interests:** specified requirements are applied prospectively in defined cases.

The exact scope and exceptions within each topic matter. Do not treat these headings as a substitute for checking the detailed paragraphs of IFRS 1 and the relevant IFRS Accounting Standards.

## Reconciliations and disclosures

A first-time adopter explains how the transition from previous GAAP to IFRS affected its reported financial position, financial performance, and cash flows. IFRS 1 requires reconciliations and related explanations, including, in the circumstances specified by the standard:

- **Equity at the date of transition:** reconcile equity reported under previous GAAP to equity under IFRS.
- **Equity at the end of the latest period presented in the most recent annual financial statements under previous GAAP:** explain the adjustments to IFRS equity at that date.
- **Total comprehensive income:** reconcile the total comprehensive income under IFRS for the latest period in the most recent annual financial statements to the amount reported under previous GAAP, or provide the required information if the entity did not report total comprehensive income under previous GAAP.
- **Material adjustments and other transition information:** explain material adjustments to the statement of financial position, statement of comprehensive income, and statement of cash flows, along with other disclosures required by IFRS 1.

The applicable disclosures depend on the entity's reporting history and circumstances; consult the current standard rather than assuming this list is complete.

## Practical review checklist

Before finalising first IFRS financial statements, consider whether the team has:

1. Confirmed the first IFRS reporting period and date of transition.
2. Prepared and reviewed the opening IFRS statement of financial position.
3. Documented the IFRS accounting policies and their consistent application across periods presented.
4. Assessed each relevant optional exemption and documented elections and supporting evidence.
5. Identified mandatory exceptions and ensured prohibited retrospective adjustments have not been made.
6. Prepared the required reconciliations, explanations, and supporting disclosures.
7. Checked that comparative information and transition disclosures are clear, internally consistent, and traceable to supporting schedules.

## Key takeaway

IFRS 1 is a structured transition framework, not simply a change in presentation. The core work is to establish the transition date, build the opening IFRS statement of financial position, apply appropriate IFRS policies, assess the standard's specific exemptions and exceptions, and clearly reconcile the effects of transition for users.
