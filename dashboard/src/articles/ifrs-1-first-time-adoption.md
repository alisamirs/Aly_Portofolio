
## What IFRS 1 is for?

IFRS 1 aims to ensure that an entity's first IFRS financial statements:

- Are transparent and comparable across periods.
- Provide a suitable starting point for future IFRS reporting.
- Deliver benefits that outweigh the cost of implementation.

It sets out how an entity moves from its previous accounting framework to IFRS Accounting Standards for its first IFRS financial statements. Its aim is to make the first IFRS information transparent and comparable across the periods presented.


## Step by Step application

| Step | Description | 
| --- | --- |
| **1. Identify the date of transition** | Determine the beginning of the earliest comparative period presented under IFRS in the first IFRS financial statements. | 
| **2. Prepare the opening IFRS statement of financial position** | Recognise, derecognise, reclassify, and measure items as required by IFRS at the transition date. |
| **3. Apply consistent IFRS accounting policies** | Use the policies that comply with IFRS and are effective at the end of the first IFRS reporting period across the periods presented, subject to IFRS 1's specific reliefs and requirements. | 
| **4. Explain the transition** | Provide the reconciliations and other disclosures required by IFRS 1. So users can understand how moving from previous GAAP affected the reported figures. |

### Worked date example

Suppose an entity presents its first IFRS financial statements for the year ending **31 December 20X5**, with one year of comparative information for **20X4**. The earliest comparative period begins on **1 January 20X4**, so that is generally the date of transition and the date of the opening IFRS statement of financial position. Always confirm the periods actually presented and the applicable IFRS 1 requirements.

## Retrospective application: the general principle

As a general starting point, an entity applies IFRS Accounting Standards retrospectively in its opening IFRS statement of financial position and throughout the periods presented, as if it had always applied IFRS. IFRS 1 modifies that principle through specific **optional exemptions** and **mandatory exceptions**. These terms are not interchangeable:

- **Optional exemptions** provide specified transition relief that a first-time adopter may elect to use. They are limited to the circumstances described in IFRS 1 and should not be extended by analogy.
- **Mandatory exceptions** prohibit retrospective application in specified areas, often to avoid hindsight or because applying a requirement retrospectively would be impracticable or inconsistent with the relevant standard's transition rules.

The lists below are illustrative rather than exhaustive; refer to the current standard for the full requirements and conditions.

## Optional exemptions

Examples of areas for which IFRS 1 provides specific optional relief include:

- **Business combinations:** specified relief from retrospective application of IFRS requirements for past business combinations.
- **Deemed cost:** in defined circumstances, an entity may elect a fair value or previous-GAAP revaluation amount as deemed cost for an eligible item at the transition date.
- **Leases:** specified transition relief for lease accounting.
- **Cumulative translation differences:** specified relief for resetting or applying accumulated translation differences.
- **Share-based payment transactions:** specified relief for certain transactions within the scope of IFRS 2.

An election is subject to detailed criteria. An exemption available for one topic does not automatically apply to other transactions or balances.

## Mandatory exceptions

IFRS 1 also requires prospective treatment in certain areas. Examples include:

- **Estimates:** estimates made under IFRS at the transition date must be consistent with estimates made for the same date under previous GAAP, after adjustments for differences in accounting policies, unless there is objective evidence of error.
- **Derecognition of financial assets and liabilities:** the transition provisions restrict retrospective application in specified circumstances.
- **Hedge accounting:** hedge accounting is subject to specific transition restrictions and conditions.
- **Non-controlling interests:** specified requirements are applied prospectively in defined cases.

The exact scope and exceptions within each topic matter. Do not treat these headings as a substitute for checking the detailed paragraphs of IFRS 1 and the relevant IFRS Accounting Standards.

## Reconciliations and disclosures

A first-time adopter explains how the transition from previous GAAP to IFRS affected its reported financial position, financial performance, and cash flows. IFRS 1 requires reconciliations and related explanations, including, in the circumstances specified by the standard:

- **Equity at the date of transition:** reconcile equity reported under previous GAAP to equity under IFRS.
- **Equity at the end of the latest period presented in the most recent annual financial statements under previous GAAP:** explain the adjustments to IFRS equity at that date.
- **Total comprehensive income:** reconcile the total comprehensive income under IFRS for the latest period in the most recent annual financial statements to the amount reported under previous GAAP, or provide the required information if the entity did not report total comprehensive income under previous GAAP.
- **Material adjustments and other transition information:** explain material adjustments to the statement of financial position, statement of comprehensive income, and statement of cash flows, along with other disclosures required by IFRS 1.

The applicable disclosures depend on the entity's reporting history and circumstances; consult the current standard rather than assuming this list is complete.

## Practical review checklist

Before finalising first IFRS financial statements, consider whether the team has:

1. Confirmed the first IFRS reporting period and date of transition.
2. Prepared and reviewed the opening IFRS statement of financial position.
3. Documented the IFRS accounting policies and their consistent application across periods presented.
4. Assessed each relevant optional exemption and documented elections and supporting evidence.
5. Identified mandatory exceptions and ensured prohibited retrospective adjustments have not been made.
6. Prepared the required reconciliations, explanations, and supporting disclosures.
7. Checked that comparative information and transition disclosures are clear, internally consistent, and traceable to supporting schedules.

## Key takeaway

IFRS 1 is a structured transition framework, not simply a change in presentation. The core work is to establish the transition date, build the opening IFRS statement of financial position, apply appropriate IFRS policies, assess the standard's specific exemptions and exceptions, and clearly reconcile the effects of transition for users.
